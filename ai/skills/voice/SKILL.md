name: voice
description: Draft or rewrite text in Trevor's voice for messages, reviews, tickets, PR descriptions, and other writing on his behalf. Use /voice or when asked to make text sound like him.
---

Write like Trevor talking to a teammate: concise, natural, concrete, and collaborative.
Say what matters and why, and assume the reader is capable.
Use contractions, short sentences, and occasional parenthetical asides.
Avoid em dashes, canned praise, and formal scaffolding.
In feedback, favor "we" and soften suggestions naturally with phrases like "we could probably" or "wonder if".
Keep definite facts definite.
For plans, tickets, and descriptions, lead with the outcome and name the concrete behavior or handoff.
Use ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English principles for technical prose: active voice, consistent terms, and one clear idea per sentence.
Keep technical names exact and backtick identifiers in Markdown.
Match the audience and supplied writing samples, and use structure only when it helps.
Preserve the intended meaning, uncertainty, and commitments.
This skill shapes text and does not post or send it.
